# Autodiff gradients through caustic crossings: literature and code search (2026-10-10)

Prepared for Jessica Lu (BAGLE finite-source JAX plan). Only items I actually opened (full text, abstract page or README) are listed as verified. "Snippet only" means I saw a search-result abstract but did not open the paper.

## Short answer
Yes, with caveats. Two published JAX codes give working autodiff gradients through binary-lens caustic crossings and have been used with NUTS on real events:
- microlux uses adaptive contour integration, custom_jvp rules and a derivative-aware error estimator.
- microJAX uses image-centred ray shooting with a smoothed JVP.

Nobody has shown a general "HMC just works across caustics" result. The published successes are single-mode, local-posterior runs (microlux, microJAX) and one degenerate-mode event (KMT-2025-BLG-1314, via a web page; the paper itself was not opened). Bartolić's thesis says caustic-crossing likelihoods are jagged and multimodal, but I could not open it.

## What I did NOT find
- No paper deriving closed-form boundary-integral derivatives of the finite-source magnification for general lenses, and no explicit "custom_jvp = boundary integral" implementation. microlux's custom_jvp is the analytic derivative of the image-position (lens-equation) map; the area derivative still comes from differentiating the discretized Green's-theorem sum. This is "differentiate through the quadrature with the right custom rules", not a separate adjoint integral.
- No literature on softening the 1/√ point-source singularity for gradient purposes. microJAX smooths only the indicator function in the JVP.
- No benchmark of HMC/NUTS acceptance or divergences on posteriors that contain several separated caustic-crossing modes. I found no HMC-across-caustics failure analysis. Bartolić's website and thesis hint at it, but I could not check the thesis.
- Nothing differentiable for FSBL with limb darkening beyond the annuli approach, and nothing for binary-source finite-source differentiable models. FSPL and binary-source differentiability are implicitly easy (sum of single-source terms), but I found no published implementation.

## My analysis (inference, not from papers)
- The finite-source magnification of a uniform disc is continuous in all parameters, including across a fold. Its first derivative is also continuous, with a square-root-type onset when the limb touches the caustic. I think the second derivative diverges there, which would matter for Hessian/mass-matrix adaptation. It matches microlux's finding that raw derivative values blow up near crossings. I have not checked this against a derivation. Limb darkening smooths it further.
- Autodiff of the contour algorithm is therefore not wrong in principle, but the discretized derivative converges more slowly than the value. Hence microlux's E^d. Contour-segment topology changes (3↔5 images) are discrete events, but the area is continuous across them, and fold-connection terms give the correct limit. So the discretization is what breaks, not the physics.

## Recommendation for BAGLE
1. **Do not differentiate through the current FSBL limb**, which grows with `jnp.insert` and adds `jax.random` noise. Use a static-shape, fixed-buffer, masked contour integrator.
2. **Most promising: microlux-style approach for FSBL/BFSBL.** Contour integration with:
   - a custom_jvp or custom_root on the polynomial roots (implicit differentiation);
   - a custom_jvp on the adaptive loop with analytic image-position derivatives;
   - `stop_gradient` on adaptive step sizes;
   - a derivative-aware error estimator near crossings;
   - assignment-based image matching.

   Strengths: proven gradients and NUTS runs, 2–3× forward cost, JIT with static buffers. Weaknesses: heavy implementation; hidden-cusp failures; the derivative estimator costs extra third derivatives; linear limb darkening only. Consider reading microlux and caustics source and porting under attribution, since the plan already says no dependency on outside code.
3. **Alternative for GPU or large sources: microJAX-style ray shooting with sigmoid-smoothed JVP.** Static shapes by construction, handles triple lenses and complex profiles. Weaknesses: biased, tunable steepness; cost scales with grid resolution; reverse mode memory-limited; CPU speed poor.
4. **A plain smooth quadrature of the point-source magnification over the disc is not supported by anything I found.** The integrand has an inverse-square-root singularity on the caustic, and fixed-node quadrature there is accurate only with very many nodes (my judgment). It is only reasonable for FSPL or far from caustics, where Gauss–Legendre or the elliptic-integral form is smooth and AD-safe.
5. **FSPL and BFSPL:** use an AD-safe single-lens quadrature (elliptic or radial-angular), since there are no topology changes. Test gradients against finite differences, with care at u≈ρ.
6. **Sampler policy:** for caustic-crossing FSBL keep nested sampling, SMC and gradient-free engines as defaults. Offer NUTS only after a gradient-vs-finite-difference test and starting from a known mode, as in microlux and microJAX. Use Fisher reparameterization and log-parameters for q, s, ρ. Use multiple chains. Plan a mode-finder (e.g. neural proposals) for multimodal events, because the published HMC results are mostly single-mode.
7. **Validation to add to the plan:** compare `jax.jacfwd` against high-precision finite differences along a light curve through fold and cusp crossings; check for spikes and NaNs; check HMC divergences at ρ = 1e-3..1e-1.
